Eight ways BoxPilot could look

Today BoxPilot looks like most server admin panels: a sidebar that lists every subsystem, the same grid of stat cards on every page, and a lot of explanatory text. Nothing tells you where to look first, and every page feels the same.

Each direction below has two screens drawn at 1040 × 600 with one realistic home server: Ubuntu 24.04, an RTX 4080, three disks, nine apps, Tailscale, and the open alert about Immich. Under each name you'll find how it navigates, how it's laid out, how it reads, who it suits, and the features it adds. Pick one, or name the parts you'd combine.

Every direction shows BoxPilot's three risk tiers somewhere on screen:

Direction 1

Launcher

Back to top ↑

A tablet-style home screen: your apps come first as big tiles with health badges, and the admin side of the server lives in a dock at the bottom.

Navigation
The home screen is your apps. Tap a tile to open the app; press and hold for its sheet (who can reach it, backups, performance, update). Admin areas sit in a dock, and one search bar reaches every app, setting and log.
Layout
A column of widgets (system, what needs a look, backups and disks) beside a five-across app grid. Deeper screens open as frosted sheets over the wallpaper instead of new pages.
Type, color, density
Figtree throughout. Frosted glass on a dusk wallpaper, 66 px icons, generous spacing and low density. Health is a dot or badge on the icon itself.
Who it suits
An owner who mostly opens BoxPilot to get to apps, and other people in the house. Less suited to long admin sessions, where the dock becomes a detour.

Signature features

  • Health badges on app icons. Immich wears an amber "!" until it has an off-box copy.
  • An app store for the catalog with a "Picked for homebox" shelf based on your GPU and free disk space.
  • Install sheet that shows where the data will live, who can reach it, and the risk tier before anything runs.
Direction 2

Command Center

Back to top ↑

A dense operations console in the spirit of Grafana and Cockpit: live metrics, an alert stack, a job queue and an inbox of pending changes, all on one screen.

Navigation
A 52 px icon rail, and a command palette (Ctrl K) that runs any registered operation by name. One time-range control drives every chart on the page.
Layout
A 12-column panel grid: metric strip, alerts, containers, action inbox, job queue, backup matrix. Everything fits on a 1080p screen without scrolling.
Type, color, density
IBM Plex Sans Condensed with JetBrains Mono for every number. Near-black panels, amber for anything that needs you, cyan for data. Roughly three times today's information per screen.
Who it suits
A power user who keeps BoxPilot open on a second monitor or a wall display. Too much for other people in the house.

Signature features

  • Action inbox: every pending change in one list, each with its tier and a single button.
  • Job queue with job numbers, live progress and durations, fed by BoxPilot's durable jobs.
  • Backup matrix: local copy, off-box copy and last drill for every app at a glance.
  • Listening vs. allowed: the firewall screen checks open ports against what is actually running.
Direction 3

Recipes & Guides

Back to top ↑

Start from what you want to get done, in plain words. Every task is a short guided recipe that lists its plan before anything runs.

Navigation
Three tabs: Do something (recipes), My box (the usual subsystem pages) and History. The search box takes whole sentences and returns recipes, not settings.
Layout
Recipe cards on the left, "Your box today" written as sentences on the right. Each recipe is a two-to-five step wizard with a live plan beside it.
Type, color, density
Bricolage Grotesque headings, Instrument Sans body. A cool light background with a soft tint per category, and plenty of room. The risk tiers get plain names: Safe, Check first, Careful.
Who it suits
First-time self-hosters and whoever runs the family server. Power users will find wizards slow for one-line edits, so My box keeps direct controls.

Signature features

  • The plan panel: what BoxPilot will do, step by step with each step's tier, before you press Start.
  • Teaching by constraint: the 4 TB disk inside homebox is greyed out as an off-box target, with the reason.
  • Resume anywhere: recipes remember their step, so "Back up my photos" waits at step 2 of 3.
Direction 4

Topology Map

Back to top ↑

The box drawn as a map: disks feed folders and shares, folders feed apps, apps pass through the firewall to your LAN and tailnet. Click anything to act on it.

Navigation
One map with lenses (Everything, Storage, Network, Backups) and layer toggles. Clicking a node opens its side panel. A List view gives the same items as a table for keyboard users and big setups.
Layout
Columns read left to right: disks, folders and shares, apps, the firewall, then LAN and tailnet devices. VMs and the GPU sit where they connect. The side panel is 320 px.
Type, color, density
Manrope with DM Mono for paths and ports. A light dot-grid canvas, colored by kind of thing: slate disks, teal folders, green GPU, orange VMs, indigo network. Medium density.
Who it suits
Visual thinkers, and anyone asking "why can't the TV see this?". It needs careful layout work to stay readable past about 40 nodes.

Signature features

  • Firewall as a wall with a labelled gate wherever traffic is allowed through, and a red dead end where it is blocked.
  • Path highlight: select Immich and its disk, folders, backup and reach light up across the whole map.
  • Storage lens with disks drawn to scale, which makes the 828 GB the Ubuntu installer left unallocated impossible to miss.
Direction 5

Timeline

Back to top ↑

BoxPilot as a readable history. Every change, job and automation run is an entry with its diff and an undo, and the home page answers "what changed since I last looked?"

Navigation
The feed is home. Filter by area or by who made the change (you, an automation, BoxPilot). "Needs you" is pinned on top. Each subsystem page is a filtered feed with that area's controls above it.
Layout
Filters on the left, the feed in the middle with diffs inline, and a digest on the right comparing now with last week. Clicking an entry opens a detail view with steps, output and rollback.
Type, color, density
Geist and Geist Mono on near-white. Color appears only on category dots, tier tags and diff lines, so the green and red of a change stand out. Medium density.
Who it suits
An owner who tinkers and wants to know what the overnight automations did. It builds directly on BoxPilot's audit trail and durable jobs.

Signature features

  • Undo on every entry, gated by the original change's tier: one click, a preview, or your password.
  • "Since last week" digest: apps, packages, firewall rules, disk growth and backup runs compared with a date you pick.
  • Change detail with the compose-file diff, each job step, and a rollback that says what it will and won't touch.
Direction 6

Copilot

Back to top ↑

Tell homebox what you want in plain words. The Ollama model already running on the box drafts a plan out of BoxPilot's own operations, each step with its risk tier, and you approve it.

Navigation
The conversation is home, with past requests on the left and live status on the right. Answers link to the classic pages ("open Firewall"), which stay available for direct edits.
Layout
Three columns: requests, the conversation with plan cards, and a live panel that becomes "what changes if you approve" while a plan is open.
Type, color, density
Sora for the interface and Source Serif 4 for the assistant's voice, so its words read differently from system text. Graphite with a peach accent. Low density.
Who it suits
An owner who knows what they want but not where the switch is. It depends on Ollama staying up and on the plan being strictly limited to registered operations.

Signature features

  • Plan cards: every step is a registered operation with its tier, and approving runs them as one recorded job.
  • Grounded answers: the model sees what's installed, so "install Jellyfin" becomes "Jellyfin is already here, I'll only add the share".
  • Morning briefing written from last night's jobs, with one-tap follow-ups.
  • Local only: the model runs on the RTX 4080, and nothing leaves homebox.
Direction 7

Pocket

Back to top ↑

Designed for the phone first: one big health status, a stack of cards that each need one decision, a bottom tab bar, and approvals straight from a push notification.

Navigation
A bottom tab bar (Home, Apps, Alerts, Box). Actions open as sheets that slide up. Push notifications go out through ntfy and carry action buttons. On a desktop the same cards reflow into a three-column grid.
Layout
A single column where every action sits in the thumb zone. Cards are ordered by what needs you, and each has one large button.
Type, color, density
Rubik, with 26 to 70 px status type. Light screens, and a single health color (green, amber or red) that carries the whole state. Very low density.
Who it suits
An owner who checks on the box from the couch or away from home over Tailscale. Deep work (LVM, firewall rules) still wants a desktop screen.

Signature features

  • A gesture per tier: tap for low, review then slide for medium, passkey plus typing the hostname for high.
  • Actionable notifications: "Install" or "Later" right on the lock screen, which then open the approval sheet.
  • Short elevation: one passkey approval unlocks high-risk actions for 10 minutes, then it locks itself.
Direction 8

Calm Settings

Back to top ↑

Make BoxPilot look like the system settings people already know from GNOME and macOS: a searchable sidebar, grouped rows with switches, and quiet dialogs. Light and dark are equally finished.

Navigation
A sidebar in four quiet groups (box, storage and network, data and machines, people and upkeep). The search field at the top finds settings inside pages, not only page names.
Layout
One centered 600 px column of grouped rows: switches, dropdowns and chevrons into sub-pages. Anything that needs a preview opens as a dialog over the page.
Type, color, density
Cantarell, GNOME's own typeface, on neutral greys with GNOME blue. Medium density and very little color, so the few amber and red marks stand out.
Who it suits
Anyone who wants BoxPilot to feel like part of Ubuntu. It is the smallest step from today's structure, and the cheapest of the eight to build.

Signature features

  • One switch per app for "reachable on my network", with the ufw rule shown in the confirmation.
  • A locked "Always reachable" group for SSH, Tailscale and BoxPilot, so nobody locks themselves out.
  • Approval policy as ordinary settings: the three tiers are rows you can read and adjust.
