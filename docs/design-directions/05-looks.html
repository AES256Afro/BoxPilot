One look for the whole site

Today Home uses the Launcher look and every other page uses the Ops console. Here is each way of making it one look, shown on Home and on a busy page (Storage).

Ops everywhere

Home moves into the console. The rail replaces the dock, the six-number strip leads, apps become a compact grid with their state in words, and the inbox sits beside them. Home and Ops turn into one page.

You gain

  • The most on one screen, at every size.
  • Nothing to redesign on the other 17 pages.

You give up

  • The wallpaper and the big app squares, the part you called charming.
  • Harder to read from across the room or at a glance on a phone.

Home everywhere

Every page gets the wallpaper, frosted glass and the dock. Tables turn into lists: a plain name first and the device path under it, big bars for how full things are, and switches for anything on or off.

You gain

  • Friendly and consistent; the whole site feels like a Mac app.
  • Easier for anyone else in the house to use.

You give up

  • Dense pages (jobs, disks, firewall rules) get about twice as long.
  • Glass everywhere is heavier on old phones; the reduced-transparency fallback has to cover every page.

Home + Ops

My pick

The Launcher is the frame: wallpaper, glass, the big friendly titles and a Mac-style sidebar that replaces both the rail and the dock. Ops supplies what goes inside: the number strip, a tier tag on every action, monospace figures and compact tables. Cyan still means a measurement and the amber mark still means something to do.

You gain

  • Keeps what you like about both, on every page.
  • One way to get around instead of a dock on Home and a rail everywhere else.

You give up

  • The most work of the three: every page's frame changes, even though the insides mostly stay.
  • Slightly less room than Ops on a small laptop, because of the sidebar.

Ten other directions

The same Home screen in ten very different looks. The tag says how much work each is: Skin changes colors, type and shapes; Skin + art also adds background art and styled controls; New layout needs its own Home screen. Tap Shortlist on the ones you like. Your picks collect in a bar at the bottom so you can copy them back to me.

1 · Aqua

Skin + art

Mac OS X, 2001. Pinstripes, gel buttons, traffic lights, a source list down the left and a dock with reflections. The most Mac-like option here. Suits: charm and nostalgia; pairs well with the Launcher.

2 · Blueprint

Skin + art

The server as a technical drawing. Apps are labelled parts, problems are numbered notes, disks get dimension lines, and the title block holds the version and the last check. Suits: people who like to see how things fit together.

3 · Phosphor

Skin

A green CRT terminal. A key letter beside every action, ASCII bars and one blinking prompt that takes typed commands. Dark only. Suits: keyboard-first use and a status screen on the wall.

4 · Rack Panel

Skin + art

Brushed-metal rack units. Each app is a module with an LED and a strip of label-maker tape, the numbers sit in red segment displays, and an amber lamp lights when something needs you. Suits: tactile fun that still reads at a glance.

5 · Swiss Poster

Skin

White, black and one red. The number of things that need you is the biggest thing on the screen. No boxes, only type and rules. Suits: calm, fast scanning, a screen in the hallway.

6 · Toybox

Skin + art

Chunky pastel cards, a face on the server that says how it's doing, and buttons that press down. Suits: family members who only need to know that it's fine and what to tap.

7 · Glass Cockpit

New layout

Avionics: four round gauges, a master caution lamp, a checklist memo and soft keys along the bottom. Set in B612, the typeface drawn for Airbus cockpit screens. Suits: watching load and temperature live.

8 · E-Ink

Skin

No color at all. Serif text, dithered bars, and sentences before numbers. Made for an e-paper frame or an old Kindle on the fridge, so status is carried by shapes and words. Suits: a low-power screen that's always on.

9 · Quest

New layout

An RPG party screen. Apps are party members with health bars, problems are quests with a reward, and homebox talks to you in a dialog box. Suits: making upkeep feel less like a chore.

10 · Transit Map

New layout

The box as a subway map. Lines are how apps are reached (your network, the tailnet) and protected (backups); a dashed line is a suspended service. The side panel reads like a line status board. Suits: seeing reach and backup coverage in one picture.

Choosing a look

A new Appearance page in Settings, drawn in the Home + Ops look. Click any look in it to select it.

How it would work

  • A look is one set of values for the tokens the stylesheet already has: colors, fonts, corner radius, glass and textures. Page code doesn't change, so adding a look doesn't touch any page.
  • Light and dark stay a separate choice. A look that only comes one way (Phosphor, E-Ink) says so under its name.
  • Picking a look applies it at once, with Keep and Go back, the way a display resolution change works.

 

  • The choice is saved to your account and follows you. A device can keep its own (a phone, a wall screen).
  • Risk tiers keep their meaning in every look. Each tier always shows its word too (low, medium, high), so a look without color stays safe.
  • Accent and Home wallpaper are small choices inside a look, so you can make Home + Ops yours without a new theme.

Suggestions

Suggested order

  1. Build Home + Ops as the default for every page. Keep the current Launcher and Ops as two more choices for anyone who prefers either.
  2. Ship the Appearance page with just those three. That proves the look switch on every page before anything new is drawn.
  3. Add the cheap skins next: Phosphor, E-Ink and Swiss Poster are colors and type only. Aqua, Blueprint, Rack Panel and Toybox follow once their art is drawn.
  4. Treat Glass Cockpit, Quest and Transit Map as other Home views, not themes, because each needs its own layout. Transit Map could also become the picture at the top of Network, which the redesign plan already wanted.

Other ideas

  • Wall screen mode. Any look, full screen, no controls, refreshed every minute. E-Ink, Swiss Poster and Glass Cockpit suit it best.
  • Contrast check per look. Run scripts/check-contrast.mjs over every look in both modes before one ships, the way it checks Home's wallpaper today.
  • Density stays separate. Comfortable and Compact work under any look, as they do now.
  • Seasonal wallpapers for Home + Ops are cheap: they're only the three glow colors.
